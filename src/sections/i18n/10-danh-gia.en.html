SECTION V.01 · OVERALL EVALUATION

Three scenarios, one overall picture.

GR00T N1.7 can control G1 to pick and place objects in simulation: 56 % on the apple task, 66 % on the 4 fruits, 66–73 % on the hammer task. Results depend first on the visual appearance of the scene and the fine-tuning data, and only then on how success is scored.

2

Overall findings

01 · VISUALS
The scene matters more than the model

With the same checkpoint and instruction, the apple task drops from 92 % (NVIDIA client, original Arena) to 56 % (only the lighting differs) and then to 0 % (the whole scene differs). A model fine-tuned on one scene does not generalize to another.

02 · FINE-TUNING
New tasks must be tuned, and tuning is fast

The fruit task scores 0 % when reusing the apple checkpoint, and the hammer task 0 % before tuning; both reach 66 % after fine-tuning. The hammer task needs only 200 episodes and ~1 hour on one H100; the main cost is the data.

03 · LANGUAGE
The model follows the instruction

0 wrong picks in 50 episodes of the fruit task, even though there are always other fruits and distractors on the table. The language part learns well from the real2sim data.

04 · BOTTLENECK
Each scenario falls short in a different phase

The apple falls short in perception and approach, the fruits in grasping, the hammer in releasing. Improvements therefore have to be task-specific; there is no single common fix.

05 · DATA
More data of the same kind has limits

Going from 200 to 400 episodes on the hammer task makes placement more accurate (hammer outside the bin interior 19 → 5 times) but the overall rate does not rise (66 % → 63 %, within the margin of error).

06 · SCORING
How success is scored affects the number

Hammer task: in 22/100 runs the hammer was already in the bin and the hand had let go, yet it was scored "not at rest" because the condition only checks instantaneous velocity. The run with the threshold relaxed to 0.02 m/s reached 73 %.

3

Lessons learned

01
Data & environment matter more than the model

Same checkpoint: 0 % or 56 % just because of the scene.

02
The instruction is part of the "contract"

Manage instructions like controlled configuration parameters.

03
Layered verification saves time

Open-loop → replay → closed-loop.

04
Success conditions need testing

Loose conditions pollute the data; overly strict conditions lower the metric. Agree on thresholds before comparing.

05
Evaluation needs large numbers

50–100 trials per configuration, always with a confidence interval.

06
Generating extra data is cheap

133 generation runs → 100 demos meeting the strict condition, with no extra teleop hours.

07
Fine-tuning is fast and cheap

~1 hour (200 episodes) to 2 h 15 (400 episodes) on 1 H100.

4

Future directions

This phase showed that GR00T N1.7 can drive the G1 to pick and place objects in simulation, and built a data → fine-tune → evaluation process for new tasks. The next steps fall into three groups: research on models and sensing, closing the visual gap between simulation and reality, then preparing to run the model on the real robot (phase 2).

01 · RESEARCH · MODELS
Research other, better models

Evaluate newer GR00T versions and other open-source VLA models (for example π0, OpenVLA) on the same three scenarios with the same scoring, to see where GR00T N1.7 stands. The standalone pipeline talks to the model through a ZMQ server, so a new model only needs its own server; the scene, runner and evaluation notebooks are reused as is.

02 · SIMULATION · IMAGES
Domain randomization of the scene

Randomize lighting, materials, background, camera and object positions when generating data (method in section III.01), so the model does not depend on one scene: the apple task drops from 92 % to 56 % from a lighting change alone. This is the groundwork for sim2real.

03 · SIMULATION · RECONSTRUCTION
3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct the real environment

Capture photos or video of the real workplace and reconstruct it with 3D Gaussian Splatting, used as the rendered background in simulation so that lighting and materials match reality; the apple task shows that a lighting change alone drops the rate from 92 % to 56 %. Combined with real2sim, training data would carry both real motion and real imagery. Gaussian Splatting only provides the images; physics still needs separate collision models for the table, shelf and objects.

04 · EVALUATION · VSS
Confirm video-based scoring

Re-run the real-time alert with top_k: 1 on fresh episodes, including robot falls, to confirm the 94.2 %; re-run video summarization and Q&A with the same fix. If it holds, this is an automatic scorer for the real robot, where there is no simulation state (section IV.05).

05 · REAL ROBOT · DATA
A combined data process

A few teleoperated demos in the actual workplace → real2sim to rebuild the scene → generate more demos and filter them in simulation → domain randomization → fine-tune, evaluate in simulation and then on the real robot (section II.04). Two of the three techniques were validated in this phase.

06 · RESEARCH · SENSING
Depth maps to help the robot judge distance

The model only receives RGB images from the head camera, while many failures come from judging distance: apples released 9–20 cm from the plate, hammers falling outside the bin or resting on its rim. Research using depth maps from the RealSense D435 on the G1 head (or depth estimated from images) as an extra observation for the model, or to check the position before release; this needs data collection and fine-tuning with depth.

Priorities: 04 takes the least effort and makes every later figure more trustworthy; 02 and 03 both target the visual gap and are prerequisites before moving to the real robot; 01 and 06 are research directions to raise the model's capability; 05 is the core of phase 2. Every further evaluation should keep this phase's practice: 50–100 trials per configuration, always with a confidence interval.
