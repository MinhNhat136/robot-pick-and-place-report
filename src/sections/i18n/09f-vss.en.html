SECTION IV.05 · VIDEO EVALUATION WITH VSS

Let a vision-language model watch the video and judge the result.

NVIDIA VSS (Video Search & Summarization) is a toolkit that analyses video with a vision-language model (VLM). This section shows how to install VSS 3.2.1 on the GPU server, then use the Isaac Sim → VSS bridge to have VSS watch hammer → bin episode videos and decide success or failure, compared with Arena's verdict. Every step has its commands, the expected result and a video of the real session.

Three ways to judge, and the results on 99 episodes

The bridge has three programs, one per VSS mechanism. Each reads a YAML config and writes a per-episode result (epNNN.json) and a summary table report.md.

MethodHow it worksTimeDecoding
Video summarization (LVS)
vss_bridge.run_pipeline		Sends the video to /v1/summarize (port 38111); the VLM writes a summary and an event timeline, and the bridge reads the verdict from the events with a strict rule~22 s / episodeDefault, with random sampling
Q&A
vss_bridge.qa_agent		The agent (port 8000) asks the VLM: "when the video ends, is the hammer fully inside the bin? YES/NO"~82 s / episodeDefault, with random sampling
Real-time alert (RT alert)
vss_bridge.rt_alert		Each episode sits in a fixed 8 s slot; the VLM (port 8018) answers "is there a failure? Yes / No"; Yes = alert = failure~0.7 s after each 8 s segment of a live streamDefault; the fix: top_k: 1

The evaluation used 99 hammer → bin episodes with Arena verdicts: 13 episodes to tune the question and how the result is read, and 86 held-out episodes used only for scoring. The baseline is always answering "success": right on 63/86 (73.3 %), because most episodes succeed. A method is only useful if it beats this baseline.

Why the fixed 8 s slots matter: successful episodes stop the moment success is reached, so their clips are shorter. In video summarization and Q&A, clip length alone would have predicted the outcome of all 86 episodes. The real-time alert gives every episode the same length, so it has no such leak; this is part of why its results are the fairest test.

Why the results varied (confirmed): with the same config and the same frames, real-time alert runs disagreed on 28 of 86 episodes; one clip sent eight times got "Yes" five times and "No" three times. The real-time VLM service passes temperature to the model only when it is not zero, so temperature 0 is dropped and the model samples at its default of 1.0. Adding top_k: 1 forces deterministic decoding: two runs then agreed on all 99 episodes, and agreement with Arena rose to 94.2 %, recognising every success and 18 of 23 failures.
94.2 % is a strong lead, not yet a result. The question was unchanged, but the run was made after the held-out outcomes were known. On the 13 tuning episodes it scored only 8 of 13 and missed both robot falls, and the held-out set contains no falls at all. It must be confirmed on a fresh run. Video summarization and Q&A suffer from the same decoding defect and have not been re-run with top_k: 1, so the comparison between the three methods is not yet fair.
  • Q&A called 31 of 63 successes failures, with answers such as "the hammer is partially inside the bin", although the final frames and Arena's measurements of the two groups are nearly identical. Likely causes: the shallow bin floor and the word "fully" in the question.
  • The VSS agent rewrote the question before passing it to the VLM in 46 of 99 cases, so the question actually asked was not fixed.

Day-to-day operation

Run these in vss/deploy/docker, with E=developer-profiles/dev-profile-lvs/generated.env.

TaskCommand
Read logsdocker logs --tail 200 vss-lvs (or vss-rtvi-vlm, vss-agent)
Stop / start the whole stack, keeping datadocker compose --env-file $E stop / start
Free the GPUdocker stop vss-rtvi-vlm; start again with docker start vss-rtvi-vlm
After a Docker daemon restartdocker start kafka phoenix (these two do not restart on their own)
Change the LLM modelEdit LLM_NAME in $E, then docker compose --env-file $E up -d --no-deps --force-recreate lvs-server vss-agent
Regular checksCall the VLM (the VLM row of the card 4 table) and df -h /. The healthcheck does not notice a dead VLM; a full disk stops Kafka and makes Elasticsearch read-only (79 GB of old data had to be cleared, with approval)
Do not run dev-profile.sh up or down on an installed machine: it deletes the 17.8 GB model volume and needs sudo. Do not cat generated.env or keys.env, and do not run docker compose config or docker inspect without a grep: they print the keys to the screen.
